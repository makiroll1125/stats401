Lab 9: Geospatial Visualization

Two views of 2025 nominal GDP for the 50 largest economies. Hover over a country to highlight it in both views.

1. Dataset Description

The dataset lists 2025 nominal GDP in billions of current U.S. dollars, plus GDP rank and ISO-3 identifier. Economies outside the top 50 have no GDP value in this dataset.

2. 2025 GDP Choropleth

Country shape shows geographic boundaries. Color shows nominal GDP. Scroll or pinch to zoom, then drag to pan.

The color scale is logarithmic. GDP ranges from about $300 billion to $30.6 trillion so a a log scale gives more visible color differences across the full range.

3. 2025 GDP Cartogram

Country polygon area represents 2025 GDP for the top 50 economies. This contiguous cartogram distorts a world map using the Cartogram Chart example from vasturiano on Github.

4. Design and Comparison

On the choropleth, the United States and China are most visually prominent because of their higher GDP and thus darker color on the map. Other countries such as Russia and Canada have large geographic sizes despite smaller GDPs so they're noticeable as well. Germany, Japan, the UK, and France have dark fills as well, but they're more difficult to notice due to their smaller geographic sizes.

In the cartogram, the United States, China, Japan, and many western European countries take up much more space. Russia, Canada, and Australia lose size as they contract due to their smaller GDP. The choropleth preserves country borders, relatively accurate geographic size, neighboring countries, and more accurate distances from each other, whereas the cartogram uses distorted area to make GDP easier to judge while preserving a somewhat recognizable world layout and positioning. Chloropleths are better for tasks such as measuring quantitative data, finding patterns, and identifying a place, however, they may struggle when region sizes vary wildly from each other as in a global map. Cartograms are more effective for visualizing quantitative data than choropleths, as well as elementary tasks such as comparison, finding the largest region, identifying a distorted region, etc. The cartogram's stretched borders and moved coastlines make actual geographic area and exact shape distorted and unreliable. However, linked highlighting helps identify a distorted country in the geographic map. Encoding GDP through area makes the concentration of output in western Europe and Japan much more easy to identify than color only.
