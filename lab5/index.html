Lab 5: Interactive Network Visualization

Dataset description

This synthetic urban transit network has 50 stations in Central, North, South, East, and West districts, and 50 undirected direct routes. Stations record daily passengers and a local, transfer, or terminal role; routes record service type and travel time.

Node-link visualization

Direct transit connections

Hover a station or route to isolate its local connections and see details. Drag stations to rearrange the force layout. District names provide weak cardinal placement, not exact geographic coordinates.

Node-link design description

District is encoded with a distinct node fill color, so geographic membership can be scanned across the topology. Weak positional forces place North, South, East, and West in their corresponding directions around Central; this reflects the broad district names without implying exact station coordinates. Daily passengers use area (a square-root size scale), making heavily used stations visually prominent without overwhelming smaller stations. Local stations are circles, transfer stations diamonds, and terminals squares because shape preserves station role even when colors are compared. Route color distinguishes Metro, Express, and Shuttle service; wider, more opaque lines indicate longer travel times. Hovering highlights stations, routes, and endpoints; tooltips supply exact values. Dragging lets the reader separate crowded crossings.

Adjacency matrix

Connections ordered by district, station type, and ID

Each filled off-diagonal cell is a direct route. Its color is route type and its opacity is travel time; diagonal cells identify each station's district. Hover a cell for the route details.

Matrix design description

Rows and columns use the same station order: district first, then station type, then station ID. The colored row and column labels and diagonal cells retain district membership, and the grouping creates five visible district blocks. Filled symmetric cells show the undirected connections. Their service color and travel-time opacity preserve the two route attributes without asking the reader to decode line length or crossing paths. This matrix is especially useful for checking whether a pair is directly connected and for spotting the long cross-district links that sit away from the diagonal; the node-link view remains better for following topology.

Network questions and findings

Question Best view Finding from the visualization
Which stations appear central? Node-link Partially answered: the most central-looking stations are the degree-3 junctions, especially s7, s16, s18, s21, s23, s31, s33, s34, and s38. They join the main spine to long cross-links; the diagram makes their three incident lines easy to see.
Which districts are strongly connected? Both Partially answered: most spine routes cross between neighboring district colors, so no same-district block dominates. The matrix's off-diagonal blocks and the node-link's long links show eight repeated neighboring-district routes plus three East-South cross-links (s4-s23, s18-s34, and s33-s44). The North-Central s7-s31 and North-West s25-s47 links are additional cross-district bridges.
Where are transfer or terminal stations? Node-link Well answered by shape: diamonds (transfer) and squares (terminal) are distributed along the spine. Several terminal squares, including s7, s10, s16, s25, s31, and s34, are also junctions; transfer stations s23, s29, and s38 likewise sit on cross-links.
Which stations have high passenger volume? Node-link Well answered by size: s50, s49, s48, s47, and s46 are the largest nodes. The view also reveals that most of these high-volume stations are peripheral or isolated rather than central in this route topology.
Where are the longest direct travel-time connections? Both Well answered: the widest/darkest Express link s7-s31 is longest at 16 minutes, followed by s18-s34 (15) and s29-s50 (14). The matching high-opacity matrix cells verify these pairs.
Are route types concentrated in particular areas? Both Partially answered: route colors alternate regularly along the main spine rather than clustering in one district. Express is the only type used by all three of the longest cross-links, while Metro and Shuttle also appear on several long cross-district connections.
