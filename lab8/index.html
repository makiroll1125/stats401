Findings from the bulletin

  1. What are the major topics? The eight major topics are arts/media, STEM/data, academic procedures, credits/degree requirements, China/Asia, society/policy/global health, university administration, and learning/community.
  2. Which topics appear across multiple formal sections? University and administration appears in 11 of 12 chapters, while learning/community appears in eight. Credits and academic procedures each span six chapters.
  3. Which formal sections contain the most semantically diverse content? Part 10, Majors and Courses, contains seven topics and has the highest topic entropy. Admission & Financial Aid and Liberal Arts Education follow at 1.78 and 1.76 bits despite having far fewer passages.
  4. Which passages from different sections are semantically similar? The Part 1 welcome on page 10 is a nearest neighbor of the Campus Programs description on page 80 since both frame DKU as a diverse intellectual community.
  5. Which passages appear unusual relative to their formal section? Several entries inside Course Descriptions sit in the credits cluster rather than a disciplinary cluster. For example, the page 243 Chinese Society and Culture requirement says its credits "do not count toward the 136-credit requirement".
  6. Search for credit, graduation, registration, or academic integrity. Does the concept occur in one semantic region or across several topics? “Credit” returns 879 passages across all eight topics. “Graduation” spans three topics with 32 passages; “registration” spans three topics with 15 passages; “academic integrity” spans three topics with 8 passages. These all occur across several topics.

Design description and methods

The official bulletin was extracted with PyMuPDF instead of pandas. PDF bookmarks provided the formal chapter, section, and subsection hierarchy, page blocks were cleaned of repeated page numbers, glyphs, whitespace, duplicates, and very short fragments. Long paragraphs were split at sentence boundaries into blocks of at most 135 words. This produced 1,333 clean passages from 3,686 raw blocks.

Each passage was encoded with sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2, producing a normalized 384-dimensional semantic vector. UMAP projected those vectors to two dimensions using 15 neighbors, minimum distance 0.15, cosine distance, and random state 401 as in the tutorial part of the lab. Kmeans clustered the original embeddings into eight groups. I inspected each cluster's highest TF-IDF terms and passages nearest its centroid then assigned the current topic labels.

In the map, position represents the UMAP projection, hue represents topic, and area represents word count. Opacity communicates current search and filter state. Zoom and pan expose dense neighborhoods, while clicking displays the complete passage and its five cosine-nearest passages. The matrix reconnects topics to the bulletin's formal chapters: rows are chapters, columns are topics, and saturation is the topic's proportion within that chapter. Coordinated interaction supports exploration by allowing users to click a matrix cell to highlights its passages on the map or select a map point to outline the corresponding cell.
